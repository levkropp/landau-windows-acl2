The classical windowed Landau theorem links a ceiling on a smoothed prime-counting signal to the exclusion of zeros in a region. It is a bridge theorem with a ceiling hypothesis, not a proof that the required ceiling exists.

Keep the quantifiers in order

Fix a constant C>0 and an exponent 0<θ<1. Suppose there are arbitrarily large starting points Y for which the ceiling Gz(x)≤C xᶿ holds throughout [Y,Y3/2].

“Arbitrarily large” means that every lower bound on the start is eventually exceeded by such a window. The same C and θ are fixed before those windows are chosen. Letting the constant change independently at each point would be a different and much weaker assumption.

Imagine a forbidden zero

The useful contrapositive begins with a zero whose real part exceeds θ. The transform of the signal has a related pole. A sufficiently isolated pole forces a contribution that conflicts with the upper estimate obtained from a successful ceiling window.

That outline hides real work: isolation, multiplicity, coefficient bounds, tails and the passage from an analytic reading to an actual finite computation. The later parts open those steps rather than treating the outline as an executable proof.

Why a one-sided estimate can be useful

A ceiling controls positive excursions of the signal. Large negative values need not violate it. The cancellation identity |v|+v=2 max(v,0) explains how a one-sided estimate can still control a suitable combined reading.

For unit weights and real phases (+1,−1,+1), a three-value instance is

|x−y+z| + (x+y+z) ≤ 2(max(x,0)+max(y,0)+max(z,0)).

At (−3,2,−1) the combined reading is four. Making the first entry minus ten still gives four, even though the oscillating magnitude alone becomes thirteen. The extra plain reading is essential.

Replace “eventually” by a computed threshold

Competing geometric scales lead to inequalities involving a base larger than one raised to an integer power. The arithmetic development computes conservative natural thresholds using rational inequalities. That computation is separate from supplying the analytic hypotheses that make those scales relevant to the actual transform.

A theorem asserting that a threshold works is stronger than a picture of one large example. It quantifies over the stated range of indices and parameters. The classical ceiling-to-zero-free theorem remains distinct from the effective certificate theorem.
