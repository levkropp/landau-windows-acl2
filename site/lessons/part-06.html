A weighted reading contains powers, factorials and exponential decay. The scalar factors can be enclosed by exact rational calculations whose precision is chosen from the final error budget.

Build an exponential band

For x≥0, write Tₖ(x)=xᵏ/k!. The recurrence T₀=1, Tₖ₊₁=Tₖ·x/(k+1) computes each term without a fresh power or factorial calculation. Let Sₙ include indices zero through n.

If 2x≤n+2, each ratio after the first omitted term is at most one half. The omitted positive tail is therefore at most twice that first omitted term. This gives the band [Sₙ,Sₙ+2Tₙ₊₁].

At x=1,n=3, the sum is 8/3, the next term is 1/24, and the band is [8/3,11/4] with full width 1/12. The tail condition is a premise, not a decoration.

Turn growth into decay

For positive endpoints, reciprocation reverses order: if L≤exp(x)≤U, then 1/U≤exp(−x)≤1/L. The example above gives [4/11,3/8].

The real exponential interpretation is supplied by a theorem. The finite band arithmetic by itself says what the endpoints and their widths are, not which analytic object they enclose.

Assemble a scalar kernel weight

The weight used in a reading has the form h·uᵏ·exp(−σu)/k!. Here h is a nonnegative cell weight, u a nonnegative address, k a natural order and σ a nonnegative damping rate.

The exact rational scale is h·Tₖ(u). Requesting primitive decay width ε/(1+scale) ensures that scaling costs at most the intended final allowance. A large scale must be paid for by tighter primitive precision.

Keep three error channels separate

Evaluation error comes from enclosing the values actually sampled. Mesh error comes from replacing the integrand on a bounded region by a finite weighted grid. Tail error comes from omitting the infinite remainder.

Making the value bands very narrow does not repair a coarse mesh or a missing tail estimate. A proof of a finite sum is not automatically a proof about an improper integral.

Show the reasoning

The first omitted term is 1/24, but there are further omitted terms. A geometric majorant with ratio one half bounds their total by 2·(1/24)=1/12. Without the ratio condition, that geometric tail argument has not been justified.
