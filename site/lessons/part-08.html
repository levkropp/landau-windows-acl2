A pole can force a coefficient too large to coexist with a ceiling-derived upper bound. The arithmetic chain is conditional on meaningful models, strict geometric gaps and checked comparisons.

Read a pole through its coefficients

The finite geometric identity gives the coefficient of zʲ in c/(a−z): it is c/aj+1. The offset must be nonzero; the analytic expansion is valid for |z|<|a|.

“Nearest” need not mean “dominant at every index.” In the teaching model 1/(2−z)−3/(3−z), coefficient zero is minus one half. After multiplication by 2j+1, the selected contribution is one while the competitor is 3(2/3)j+1. At index five the result is 179/243>1/2.

Create a strict isolation gap

For a finite set of rational points left of a fixed center line, equality of two squared distances gives a linear equation in the center height. There are finitely many candidate tie heights. Repeated interval halving avoids a supplied finite list and returns a rational midpoint.

In the film's point example, the chosen height is 5/8. The squared distances are 73/64,233/64,281/64. Averaging the nearest distance with the next one gives squared radius 153/64, strictly inside the gap.

Actual zeros require more than rational markers

An actual zero need not have rational coordinates. A box with rational endpoints encloses uncertainty. Interval subtraction, careful interval squaring and addition bound every squared distance from the center to that box.

The circle checker requires the selected box's upper bound to be below the squared radius and every other box's lower bound to be above it. Its parameter named radius stores a squared radius.

Checking a cover does not generate one

To isolate an actual zero, we need proof that every relevant zero is covered and that the selected box contains only the selected zero location. A unique location can have multiplicity greater than one. A cluster of distinct locations is a different issue.

A missing box could hide another zero inside the circle even when all supplied boxes pass. The real geometry theorem retains the complete-cover and uniqueness hypotheses explicitly.

Compare constructed models and retain the real anchor

The model polynomial adds the selected pole coefficient, the other listed poles and a rescaled regular coefficient. A boundary comparison with the polynomial from the actual Mellin evaluator controls their selected coefficient difference. Agreement is checked, not assumed.

The one-sided estimate also retains a height-zero plain real reading. A separate model bounds how negative it can be. Only with that anchor can the failed-search inequality produce the required upper bound for the oscillating reading.

Geometric thresholds choose a large index; explicit tolerances pay for comparison errors. The caps must be compatible uniformly as precision changes. A cap that depends circularly on the requested precision cannot be fixed merely by naming a threshold.

What the conditional conclusion returns

Once the model and budget checks pass, lower and upper bounds for the same normalized reading cannot coexist under search failure. The conditional witness theorem therefore returns an actual finite-search result and a rational address x, with Y≤x, x²≤Y³, and a positive lower signal endpoint.
